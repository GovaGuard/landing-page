EUCS: the EU cloud certification scheme that still isn't here

EUCS has not been adopted. Seven years after the Cybersecurity Act created the EU certification framework, the cloud scheme ENISA drafted in December 2020 remains a candidate. The contested sovereignty requirements were removed in the March 2024 draft; formal adoption never followed. On 20 January 2026 the Commission published a proposal to recast the Cybersecurity Act, reopening the framework itself. This page tracks the status.

EUCS (European Cybersecurity Certification Scheme for Cloud Services) is the EU's draft certification scheme for cloud providers under the Cybersecurity Act (Regulation (EU) 2019/881). Drafted by ENISA since 2020, it defines the assurance levels basic, substantial and high – but as of September 2026 it has not been formally adopted and confers no legal effect.

By Nils Carstensen & Manuel Eckarth · Last reviewed: September 17, 2026

The fight that stalled EUCS was never about cryptography or logging. It was about whether the level “high” should require providers to be headquartered in the EU and immune from non-EU law – a model championed by France, built on its SecNumCloud scheme, and opposed by member states including Ireland, Sweden and the Netherlands, as well as by non-EU hyperscalers.

// WHERE IT STANDS

The verified status, September 2026

Fact: EUCS remains a candidate scheme. The March 2024 draft – the last one publicly known – dropped the EU-headquarters and data-localization requirements in favour of a transparency attestation, in which providers declare the non-EU jurisdictions they are subject to and a conformity assessment body verifies the declaration. That draft has sat with the European Cybersecurity Certification Group and the Commission ever since.

Fact: the Commission's Cybersecurity Act recast proposal of 20 January 2026 keeps certification voluntary but overhauls the machinery – ENISA must deliver a candidate scheme within twelve months of a Commission request, and certified entities may gain a presumption of conformity with obligations such as NIS2. The proposal does not adopt EUCS, and it is itself only at the start of the ordinary legislative procedure.

Expectation, clearly labeled as such: most observers read the recast as the Commission's attempt to unblock stalled schemes like EUCS rather than abandon them. Whether EUCS is adopted under the current rules, re-scoped under the new ones, or overtaken by sector-specific sovereignty rules in member states is genuinely open. We will not predict an adoption date, because no institution has published one.

// GERMANY

What adoption would mean for German cloud providers

If EUCS is adopted, Article 57 of the Cybersecurity Act kicks in: national certification schemes covered by a European scheme cease to produce effects, and member states stop introducing new national schemes for the same scope. Existing national certificates remain valid until they expire. In Germany, the scheme in that blast radius is BSI C5 – the catalogue EUCS itself drew on, alongside SecNumCloud.

Expectation, not fact: adopted schemes so far suggest generous transition arrangements. The EUCC gave national predecessor schemes a transition period before certification switched over, and the EUCS drafts have consistently foreseen phased entry by assurance level. How C5 attestations would map onto EUCS levels – and over what period – will only be knowable from the adopted implementing act.

What is fact today: C5 is the German benchmark, and it is the scheme German law actually names. Section 393 SGB V requires cloud services processing social and health data to hold a current C5 attestation – it says C5, not EUCS. A C5-grade control set is therefore the no-regret move: it wins German deals now and positions you for whatever EUCS becomes. How we build that is described in our C5 practice (German).

// FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Has EUCS been adopted?

No. As of September 2026, EUCS remains a candidate scheme without legal effect. The only European cybersecurity certification scheme adopted so far is the EUCC for ICT products (Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/482, adopted 31 January 2024). No adoption date for EUCS has been announced by the Commission or ENISA.

What happened to the sovereignty requirements?

Earlier drafts required, for the level “high”, EU headquarters, immunity from non-EU law and EU data localization. The draft of 22 March 2024 removed them, replacing them with a transparency attestation: providers declare the non-EU jurisdictions they are subject to, verified by the assessment body. France continues to pursue sovereignty nationally via SecNumCloud and sector rules.

Will EUCS replace BSI C5?

Eventually, if adopted: under Article 57 of the Cybersecurity Act, national schemes covered by a European scheme cease to produce effects, with existing certificates valid until expiry and transition arrangements expected. Until then, C5 is fully valid – and § 393 SGB V names C5, not EUCS, so German health-sector deals require C5 today regardless.

Does the January 2026 Cybersecurity Act proposal adopt EUCS?

No. The recast proposal of 20 January 2026 reforms the framework itself: ENISA must prepare candidate schemes within twelve months of a request, and certification may carry a presumption of conformity with rules such as NIS2. It is a proposal at the start of the legislative procedure; EUCS remains a separate, still-unadopted candidate scheme.
