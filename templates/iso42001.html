ISO 42001: AI governance your buyers can verify

Enterprise buyers have started asking how you govern AI — in security questionnaires, in DPAs, and now in EU AI Act compliance checks. Most vendors answer with a policy PDF written last quarter.

ISO/IEC 42001 is the certifiable answer: an audited AI management system (AIMS) that a third party has actually verified. And because it shares its structure with ISO 27001, it bolts onto an existing ISMS instead of starting from zero. Right now the field of certified vendors is thin. That is precisely the moment to hold the certificate.

// SCOPE

What ISO 42001 covers

ISO/IEC 42001 is a management system standard — the same Annex SL structure as ISO 27001 — for the responsible development, provision, and use of AI. It requires an AI policy, assigned roles and responsibilities, AI system impact assessments, lifecycle controls across data, model development, deployment, and monitoring, supplier AI management, and a continual improvement loop.

One distinction matters: the certificate attests to your governance of AI, not to the models themselves. No auditor certifies that a model is accurate or unbiased; they certify that you have a working system for assessing, deciding, and monitoring — which is what buyers and regulators actually ask for.

// INTEGRATION

Bolting onto ISO 27001

Because both standards share the identical high-level structure, the shared clauses — context, leadership, planning, support, performance evaluation — are literally reusable: same risk method, same document control, same competence records. The AI-specific delta is Annex A of 42001: AI impact assessments, data governance for training data, transparency and human-oversight controls.

For a company with a certified ISMS, that delta is an extension project, not a second program, and an integrated audit covers both standards under one management review cadence. This is how we build it in our ISO 27001 practice: one system, two certificates.

// FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Does ISO 42001 make us EU AI Act compliant?

No certificate grants automatic conformity with the Act. What ISO 42001 does is operationalize the risk-management, documentation, and oversight discipline the Act expects — and the harmonized standards under development are converging in the same direction. Treat the certificate as strong evidence, not a legal shield.

Do we need ISO 27001 first?

Not formally — 42001 stands on its own. Practically, yes: it assumes information-security fundamentals like access control, logging, and supplier management. With an existing ISMS the delta is measured in months; without one you are building two management systems at once, and the sequencing almost always favors 27001 first.

We only use third-party LLMs — is this relevant?

Yes. ISO 42001 covers the deployer side explicitly: acceptable-use policies, human oversight of AI-assisted decisions, supplier management for the model vendors you depend on, and impact assessments for consequential use cases. Most of the AI governance questions in vendor assessments target exactly this deployer role.

How long does certification take?

With an existing ISO 27001 ISMS, typically three to five months to audit-ready: the shared clauses already exist, so the work is the AI-specific Annex A delta. Standalone, expect something closer to the ISO 27001 timeline of four to eight months, because you are building the management system foundation as well.
