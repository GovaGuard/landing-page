// INSIGHTS · ISO 27001

Choosing an ISO 27001 certification body (and spotting cert mills)

ISO itself does not issue certificates – the standards organization says so explicitly. Independent certification bodies audit and certify, and their accreditation decides whether your customers accept the result. For SMEs, expect roughly €5,000–€15,000 including surveillance audits over the three-year cycle. This guide covers how to pick a body – and how to recognize the ones selling paper.

Accredited certification body is an audit organization whose competence to certify against ISO/IEC 27001 has been verified by a national accreditation body – DAkkS in Germany, UKAS in the UK, ANAB in the US – against ISO/IEC 17021-1 and ISO/IEC 27006. Accreditation, not the auditor’s brand, is what makes a certificate accepted internationally.

By Nils Carstensen & Manuel Eckarth · Last reviewed: September 17, 2026

// ACCREDITATION

Why accreditation decides whether your certificate counts

The chain has three links. At the top sits the International Accreditation Forum (IAF), whose members recognize each other’s work under a multilateral agreement. Its members are national accreditation bodies – one per country, such as DAkkS in Germany, UKAS in the UK, ANAB in the US. They in turn accredit the certification bodies that audit companies like yours.

That chain is the entire value of the certificate. When an enterprise procurement team accepts your ISO 27001 certificate instead of sending a 300-question security questionnaire, they are trusting the accreditation behind it – not the PDF. A certificate from a body outside this chain verifies nothing, and experienced buyers reject it.

The audit itself runs Stage 1 (documentation review) plus Stage 2 (implementation audit), then annual surveillance audits across a three-year cycle – the process our ISO 27001 practice prepares clients for.

Weight the criteria by your sales reality: a Munich industrial supplier needs a different brand than a SaaS company selling into US enterprises. Accreditation is the only non-negotiable on the list.

// CERT MILLS

Spotting certificate mills

Certificate mills sell ISO 27001 “certificates” without an accreditation traceable to an IAF member. The tells are consistent: no Stage 1 / Stage 2 distinction, a certificate issued days after payment, sometimes without any audit at all, and “lifetime” validity with no surveillance audits. The price looks unbeatable because the product is worthless.

Verification takes two minutes: IAF CertSearch is the official global database of accredited management system certifications, fed directly by accreditation and certification bodies. Any genuine, accredited ISO 27001 certificate – yours or a supplier’s – should be findable there. For Germany, DAkkS publishes its accredited certification bodies; if a body claims DAkkS accreditation, the claim is checkable at the source.

// PROCESS

Four steps from longlist to contract

  1. Shortlist accredited candidates

    Start from your accreditation body’s register, not from search ads. Filter for ISO/IEC 27001 in the accreditation scope and for demonstrable experience in your industry. Three to five candidates are enough for real comparison.

  2. Request quotes on a defined scope

    Send every candidate the same scope statement: locations, headcount, services, and the control set from your Statement of Applicability – see the Statement of Applicability guide. Identical inputs are the only way quotes become comparable.

  3. Ask for the lead auditor’s CV

    Reputable bodies share the proposed lead auditor’s background on request. You are buying three years with this person – check for experience with your technology and, if relevant, your language and regulatory environment.

  4. Contract with the three-year view

    Compare total cost across the full cycle: Stage 1, Stage 2, two surveillance audits, travel, and certificate fees. A cheap year one with expensive surveillance audits is the oldest pricing trick in the market.

// FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Does it matter which certification body issues my ISO 27001 certificate?

Yes – but the binary question is accreditation, not brand. A certificate from any body accredited by an IAF-member accreditation body (DAkkS, UKAS, ANAB and peers) is internationally recognized. An unaccredited certificate is routinely rejected in enterprise vendor reviews. Among accredited bodies, brand weight matters mainly where your specific buyers expect a particular logo.

How do I verify that an ISO 27001 certificate is genuine?

Search it in IAF CertSearch (iafcertsearch.org), the official global database of accredited management system certifications. It confirms the certificate, the certification body, and the accreditation behind it in one lookup. If a certificate is not findable and the issuer cannot name an IAF-member accreditation body, treat it as unverified.

What does an ISO 27001 certification body cost?

For SMEs, roughly €5,000–€15,000 including the annual surveillance audits in years two and three. The initial certification consists of Stage 1 (documentation review) and Stage 2 (implementation audit); the certificate is valid for three years before recertification. Always compare quotes on the full three-year total, not the year-one price.

Can my ISO 27001 consultant also certify my ISMS?

No. Accreditation rules (ISO/IEC 17021-1) require impartiality: a body cannot certify a management system it helped design or implement. Anyone offering to build and certify your ISMS in one package is describing a conflict of interest – and usually a certificate mill. Consulting and certification must come from independent parties.
