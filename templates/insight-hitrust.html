HITRUST e1, i1 or r2: which assessment do you actually need?

HITRUST offers three certifiable assessment tiers: e1 with 43 controls on an annual cycle, i1 with 182 controls on an annual cycle, and r2 with a control set tailored to your risk profile, valid for two years with an interim assessment after year one – per hitrustalliance.net. All three require a HITRUST Authorized External Assessor for the validated assessment. The right tier is not a maturity question; it is a contract question.

This guide compares the tiers, walks the selection ladder, and names the traps. For the US health-tech context – safeguards, the BAA chain, and where HITRUST fits – start with our HIPAA & HITRUST readiness page and the BAA guide.

HITRUST CSF is a certifiable security and privacy framework that harmonizes controls from HIPAA, NIST, ISO 27001 and dozens of other authoritative sources into one control set. Organizations prove their posture through three assessment tiers – e1, i1 and r2 – validated by an authorized external assessor and certified by HITRUST itself.

By Nils Carstensen & Manuel Eckarth · Last reviewed: September 17, 2026

// CONTEXT

Why HITRUST exists at all

HIPAA has no official certification. HHS certifies nothing and endorses no third-party seal – you demonstrate compliance through a documented risk analysis and implemented safeguards. That leaves hospital and payer procurement with a problem: how to compare vendor claims without auditing every vendor themselves.

HITRUST exists precisely because buyers wanted a certifiable proxy for HIPAA posture: an assessor-validated report against a harmonized control set, scored and certified by a central body. When a US health-care contract says “HITRUST certified”, it is outsourcing vendor due diligence to that mechanism – and naming the tier it will accept.

The tiers nest: i1 contains the e1 controls, and HITRUST lets work from a lower assessment carry into a more comprehensive one. Moving up later is a planned upgrade, not a restart – which is exactly why you should buy the tier your current deal requires, not the one your ambition suggests.

// SELECTION

The selection ladder: let the deal decide

  1. Read what the contract requires

    Before pricing anything, read the security exhibit of the deal driving this. “HITRUST certified” without a tier, “i1 or higher”, and “r2 validated assessment” are three different projects. If the language is ambiguous, ask the counterparty's procurement team – in writing – which report they will accept.

  2. e1 as first signal – only if procurement accepts it

    The 43-control e1 works as an entry signal for smaller deals and early diligence. It is fast and affordable, but some buyers treat it as insufficient. Confirm acceptance before you buy it; an e1 nobody accepts is the most expensive tier of all.

  3. i1 as the SaaS workhorse

    For most SaaS vendors selling into US health care, the 182-control i1 is the sweet spot: annual cycle, threat-adaptive control set, accepted by most mid-market procurement. If no contract names r2, the i1 is usually where the cost-to-acceptance ratio peaks.

  4. r2 when a hospital system writes it into the MSA

    The r2 is the heavyweight hospitals ask for: risk-based scoping, the deepest evidence demands, two-year validity with an interim assessment. Commit to it when a signed or near-signed contract requires it – and start where your largest deal requires, because the tiers build on each other.

// REUSE

You are not starting from zero

The HITRUST CSF is deliberately a harmonization: it maps controls from ISO 27001, NIST and other sources into one framework. If you already run an ISO 27001 ISMS or hold a SOC 2 report, that control set maps substantially into the CSF – policies, access management, logging, vendor management and incident response largely carry over.

That is the same one-control-set logic we apply across every framework: build one control environment, then produce another output from it. A HITRUST tier becomes an additional report from the machinery you already operate – plus the CSF-specific scoping, scoring and evidence formatting the assessor will expect.

// TRAPS

Two traps that burn budgets

Buying r2 ambition with an e1 budget. Teams hear “hospitals want r2” and scope for it before any contract demands it – then stall mid-project when the tailored control count and evidence depth outrun the budget. A finished i1 beats an abandoned r2 in every procurement conversation. Upgrade when a deal pays for it.

Forgetting the assessor line item. A validated assessment – the thing that produces a certification – must be performed by a HITRUST Authorized External Assessor, on top of HITRUST's own fees and your internal effort. A self-run readiness assessment is preparation, not certification. Plan the assessor cost into the tier decision from day one.

// FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Does HITRUST certification replace HIPAA compliance?

No. HIPAA compliance is a legal obligation you demonstrate through a documented risk analysis and implemented safeguards; HHS certifies nothing. HITRUST is the certifiable proxy buyers accept when they want an assessor-validated report of that posture. You still need both: the HIPAA program underneath, HITRUST as its marketable output.

Do I need an external assessor for HITRUST?

For certification, yes. A validated assessment at any tier – e1, i1 or r2 – must be performed by a HITRUST Authorized External Assessor before HITRUST issues the certification. You can run a readiness assessment yourself to find gaps first, but a readiness result is not a certificate and no buyer treats it as one.

How long is each HITRUST certification valid?

e1 and i1 certifications are valid for one year and renewed annually. The r2 certification is valid for two years, with an interim assessment after year one to confirm the controls still operate. Factor the renewal rhythm into cost comparisons: an annual i1 and a two-year r2 price very differently over a three-year horizon.

Can I reuse my ISO 27001 or SOC 2 work for HITRUST?

Substantially, yes. The HITRUST CSF harmonizes controls from ISO 27001, NIST and other sources, so an existing ISMS or SOC 2 control set maps into large parts of any tier. What remains is HITRUST-specific: CSF scoping, the scoring model, and evidence in the shape the external assessor expects.
