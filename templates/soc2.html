// MARKET REALITY

Why US buyers demand SOC 2

01

Procurement reality

US enterprises ask for the report, not a certificate. Their vendor risk process has a field for it, and the field is mandatory. No SOC 2, no vendor onboarding — the deal doesn't die loudly, it just stops moving.

  • Standard vendor risk requirement
  • Unblocks stalled security reviews
  • Expected, not exceptional, for SaaS
02

A report, not a cert

SOC 2 is an AICPA attestation issued by a licensed CPA firm — a detailed report with restricted distribution, not a logo for your footer. Your customer's security team reads it page by page: system description, controls, test results, exceptions.

  • AICPA attestation standard
  • Issued by a licensed CPA firm
  • Read closely, so quality matters
03

Trust service criteria

Security is mandatory; Availability, Confidentiality, Processing Integrity, and Privacy are optional add-ons. Every extra criterion adds controls, evidence, and audit cost. Scope what your customers actually ask for — usually Security plus Availability — and nothing else.

  • Security: always in scope
  • Optional criteria on customer demand
  • Tight scope keeps cost sane
// REPORT TYPES

Type I vs Type II

Type I Type II
What it attests Design of controls at a single point in time Operating effectiveness over an observation window
Time horizon One day — a snapshot 3–12 months of evidence
Signal to buyers Weak; often rejected by mature security teams What enterprise buyers actually want
When it makes sense A fast placeholder while the Type II window runs The report that closes deals

The pragmatic path: readiness work first, then a short first observation window — three months — for your initial Type II, then annual twelve-month cycles thereafter. Skipping straight to a Type I rarely buys you anything a serious buyer will accept.

// LEVERAGE

The ISO 27001 overlap advantage

The control overlap between ISO 27001 and SOC 2 is substantial: access control, change management, incident response, and vendor management map nearly one to one. If you already operate an ISMS, it covers the bulk of the Security criteria before the SOC 2 project even starts.

The real delta is US-specific mechanics — the system description the CPA firm attests, evidence produced at attestation depth rather than certification depth, and the discipline of controls running cleanly through an entire observation window.

We map your existing control set once and make both frameworks draw from it, instead of running a second program in parallel. See SOC 2 vs ISO 27001 — the full comparison for the framework-by-framework breakdown.

// EXECUTION

The path to your report

  1. Readiness assessment

    Scope the trust service criteria against what your customers actually require, map your existing controls — including anything your ISO 27001 ISMS already covers — and produce a concrete gap list with owners and a timeline.

  2. Remediation & evidence automation

    Close the gaps. This is where GRC tooling like Vanta or Drata genuinely shines — SOC 2 evidence collection is exactly what these platforms were built for. Useful, not sufficient: the tooling gathers evidence, it does not design your controls or write your system description.

  3. Observation window

    The controls run and generate evidence for three to twelve months. Every skipped access review and undocumented change becomes an exception in the report — we keep the controls on the rails so the window produces a clean record instead of a list of caveats.

  4. Audit & report

    We help you select a CPA firm that fits your size and budget, support fieldwork — interviews, evidence requests, walkthroughs — and handle exceptions until the report is issued and ready to hand to your buyers.

// FAQ

Frequently asked questions

How long does SOC 2 take?

Readiness takes two to three months, the observation window runs three to twelve months, and the audit itself takes roughly one month. With a short first window, a Type II report inside six to seven months from a standing start is realistic. Teams with an existing ISO 27001 ISMS compress the readiness phase considerably.

What does SOC 2 cost?

The CPA firm audit typically runs $20k–$60k for SMEs, depending on scope, the number of trust service criteria, and the length of the observation window. On top of that: readiness consulting and, optionally, GRC tooling for evidence automation. Extra criteria beyond Security add audit hours — scope deliberately.

Do we need SOC 2 AND ISO 27001?

If you sell on both sides of the Atlantic: increasingly yes. European buyers ask for the ISO 27001 certificate, US buyers ask for the SOC 2 report. The point is to build both on one control set — then the second framework is an incremental effort on top of the first, not a second security program.

Can a German company get SOC 2?

Yes. SOC 2 has no geography requirement — any licensed CPA firm can attest a European company, and remote fieldwork is standard practice. Plenty of German and EU SaaS companies hold SOC 2 Type II reports precisely because their US customers demand them.
