// REGULATION

Cyber Resilience Act: CE marking comes to software

Since 11 September 2026, manufacturers must report actively exploited vulnerabilities in their products to ENISA within 24 hours. That obligation is live now — it started days ago. And from 11 December 2027, no product with digital elements enters the EU market without CRA conformity and a CE mark.

Software vendors have never faced product regulation like this: not a management system standard, not a customer questionnaire, but market-access law with the same machinery that governs medical devices and machinery. If you ship software into the EU, the clock is already running.

Reviewed by Nils Carstensen & Manuel Eckarth · September 2026

// SCOPE

Who and what is covered

01

Products with digital elements

The CRA covers virtually every "product with digital elements" made available on the EU market: hardware with embedded software, standalone software, IoT devices. Pure SaaS is out of scope — unless it qualifies as a remote data processing solution integral to a product, in which case it comes right back in.

  • Hardware with software
  • Standalone software products
  • IoT and connected devices
02

Product classes decide the route

Default-class products self-assess conformity. Important products in Class I and II — password managers, firewalls, hypervisors and similar — face stricter conformity routes. Critical products require EU certification. Classification determines cost, timeline, and whether you need a notified body at all.

  • Default: self-assessment
  • Important Class I & II: stricter routes
  • Critical: EU certification
03

Open source is not a free pass

The CRA carves out non-commercial open-source development and creates the lighter "open-source steward" role for foundations. But commercial activity around OSS — monetized distributions, paid support, shipping it inside your product — still triggers full manufacturer duties. That boundary needs a scoping call, not assumptions.

  • Non-commercial contribution exempt
  • Steward regime for foundations
  • Monetization triggers duties
// DEADLINES

The dates that matter

Date What applies
10 December 2024 Entry into force
11 June 2026 Notified-body framework applies — conformity assessors become bookable, and their capacity is finite
11 September 2026 Reporting obligations LIVE: actively exploited vulnerabilities and severe incidents to ENISA — early warning within 24 hours, full notification within 72 hours. Applies to products already on the market
11 December 2027 Full obligations: essential cybersecurity requirements, technical documentation, CE marking. Non-conforming products can no longer be placed on the EU market

The September 2026 date is the one teams underestimated. Everyone planned for December 2027; the reporting duty arrived fifteen months earlier — and if you cannot detect exploitation in your product, you cannot report it in 24 hours.

// OBLIGATIONS

What manufacturers must build

  1. Secure-by-design development

    The essential requirements in Annex I Part I of the regulation: products ship with no known exploitable vulnerabilities, secure-by-default configuration, and a minimized attack surface. This is engineering-process regulation — it has to hold at every release, not once at certification.

  2. Vulnerability handling process

    Annex I Part II: an SBOM for every product, a coordinated vulnerability disclosure policy, and security updates for the entire support period — at least five years where users can reasonably expect it.

  3. Reporting capability

    A working 24-hour / 72-hour pipeline to ENISA for actively exploited vulnerabilities and severe incidents, plus notification of affected users. Detection, triage, and submission have to function under real incident pressure — this obligation is already binding.

  4. Technical documentation & conformity

    A cybersecurity risk assessment per product and the technical file that backs the CE mark — assessed via self-assessment or a notified body, depending on product class.

// EXECUTION

The engagement path

  1. Product scoping & classification

    Which products are in scope, which class each falls into, which conformity route follows — the decision everything else hangs on, and the one to get right before spending on anything downstream.

  2. Gap assessment against Annex I

    We map your secure development practices, vulnerability handling, and support-period policy against the essential requirements and produce a prioritized backlog per product.

  3. Process build

    SBOM generation wired into CI, a published coordinated disclosure policy, and an ENISA reporting runbook connected to your incident response — so the 24-hour clock is survivable, not theoretical.

  4. Documentation & conformity support

    The technical file, the self-assessment declaration of conformity, or preparation for the notified-body route where classification requires it.

An ISO 27001 ISMS provides the organizational chassis — see our ISO 27001 practice — but the CRA is product regulation and demands product-level engineering evidence on top.

// FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Does the CRA apply to pure SaaS?

Mostly no — pure SaaS falls under NIS2 instead. But embedded agents, on-prem components, mobile apps, and "remote data processing solutions" integral to a product are in scope. Architecture decides, so map your product boundaries precisely before assuming exemption.

We ship open source — are we exempt?

Contributing to open source is not commercial activity and carries no CRA duties. Monetizing a distribution, offering paid support, or shipping OSS inside your product is commercial activity — and makes you a manufacturer. The lighter steward regime covers foundations, not vendors.

What happens if we miss December 2027?

No CE mark means no EU market access for new placements. Market-surveillance authorities can order withdrawal of non-conforming products, and fines reach €15 million or 2.5% of global annual turnover, whichever is higher.

Do we need a notified body?

Default-class products self-assess — that covers most software. Important Class I products can self-assess only by applying the harmonized standards in full; Class II and Critical products need third parties. Classifying early means assessor capacity gets booked early — it is finite, and the queue will not be.
