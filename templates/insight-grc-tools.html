GRC platforms: what Vanta, Drata & Co. do brilliantly — and what they can't do

GRC platforms automate evidence collection, monitor your integrations around the clock, and give auditors a tidy place to look. What they do not do: perform your risk assessment, write your Statement of Applicability, or run your management review. Those remain human work under ISO/IEC 27001 — and they are exactly what a certification auditor asks about in the Stage 2 interview. This article draws the line fairly: what the subscription buys, and what it never claimed to.

GRC platform is software for governance, risk and compliance that connects to your cloud, HR and developer tooling, continuously collects evidence against control frameworks such as SOC 2 or ISO 27001, flags failing checks, and presents the results in dashboards for teams and auditors. Vanta, Drata, Secureframe and Sprinto are well-known examples.

By Nils Carstensen & Manuel Eckarth · Last reviewed: September 17, 2026

// CREDIT WHERE DUE

What these platforms genuinely do well

Evidence automation is the headline feature, and it is real. Screenshots of MFA settings, access reviews exported by hand, ticket samples pulled the week before the audit — a platform replaces that grind with API integrations that collect evidence continuously and timestamp it. For a SOC 2 Type II observation period, where you must show controls operated for months, that is a substantial saving.

Integration monitoring is the second honest win. When an employee is offboarded in your HR system but keeps a GitHub seat, or a production bucket loses its encryption flag, the platform notices within hours rather than at the annual review. Drift detection of this kind is something no manual process does as reliably.

Policy templates and auditor dashboards round it out. Templates are legitimate starting points — far better than a blank page, provided you edit them until they describe your company. And a read-only auditor view with mapped evidence shortens fieldwork noticeably. We work alongside these platforms in most engagements; this is not a case against buying one.

// THE DIVIDING LINE

ISMS activities: platform, human, auditor

The pattern below is consistent: tooling accelerates evidence collection; it does not perform your risk assessment, write your SoA, or run your management review. The third column matters most — it is who sits in the interview chair.

ISMS activity What the platform provides What remains human work Who your auditor will ask
Risk assessment A risk register module and generic risk libraries Identifying your actual risks, choosing a methodology, scoring and treating them Management and the ISMS owner
Statement of Applicability A pre-mapped list of Annex A controls Justifying every inclusion and exclusion from your risk results The ISMS owner, in detail
Control design decisions Default checks and recommended configurations Deciding what is proportionate for your scope, and documenting why Control owners
Management review Dashboards and metrics as input material Leadership actually reviewing the ISMS and deciding on resources Top management, directly
Internal audit Evidence the internal auditor can sample An independent, competent audit against the standard — the tool cannot audit itself The internal auditor
Audit interviews Nothing — interviews are conversations Staff who can explain the process they follow, unprompted Anyone in scope, sampled
Incident judgment calls Alerts, tickets and a documented workflow Severity classification, disclosure decisions, lessons learned The incident response lead
// FAILURE MODE

Tool-green is not audit-ready

The recurring failure is not the platform — it is the expectation that a subscription replaces the work. The dashboard sits at 95%, every integration is green, and then the Stage 2 interview begins: the auditor asks how the risk methodology was chosen, why control 8.16 is excluded, and when leadership last reviewed the ISMS. Nobody in the room can answer, because nobody performed those activities. The tool collected evidence for a system that was never actually operated.

Certification auditors have seen a thousand template policies. What they probe is whether the ISMS is lived: decisions with owners, reviews with minutes, risk treatments with dates. A green dashboard is evidence of connected APIs, not of judgment. The same gap shows up in the paper-ISMS warning signs — a purchased tool can produce paper at higher speed.

// BUYING DECISION

When a platform earns its price

Buy one when the evidence volume justifies it. For SOC 2 — especially a Type II with a long observation window and many cloud integrations — the automation pays for itself in saved engineering hours, and auditors increasingly expect the format. The AICPA's SOC 2 framework is evidence-heavy by design; that is the platforms' home turf.

For a first, small-scope ISO 27001 certification the case is weaker. The bulk of the effort is thinking work — scope, risk assessment, SoA, getting leadership to own the ISMS — which the platform does not shorten. At €10k–40k per year, a small company can reasonably defer the purchase to year two, once the ISMS exists and continuous evidence becomes the bottleneck.

Our ISO 27001 practice works with your platform when you have one, and without one when you don't — the standard requires neither.

// FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Can Vanta or Drata get me ISO 27001 certified on their own?

No. They collect evidence and provide templates, but certification requires activities only people can perform: a risk assessment, a justified Statement of Applicability, management review, and an independent internal audit. Auditors interview your team about these in Stage 2. The platform accelerates the evidence side; it cannot stand in for the judgment side.

Is a GRC platform mandatory for SOC 2 or ISO 27001?

No framework requires one. SOC 2 and ISO 27001 audits accept evidence in any credible form — spreadsheets, exports, minutes. Platforms are genuinely useful at SOC 2 evidence scale, where continuous collection over an observation period saves real hours. For a first small-scope ISO 27001, manual evidence handling is often perfectly workable.

My compliance dashboard shows 95% — am I audit-ready?

Not necessarily. The percentage measures passing automated checks, not whether your ISMS is operated. Auditors test the parts no integration can see: how risks were assessed, why controls were excluded, whether leadership reviews the system. If those questions have no answers, a green dashboard will not save the audit.

Do consultants compete with GRC platforms?

They complement each other. The platform automates evidence collection and monitoring; consultants supply the judgment work — risk methodology, SoA decisions, audit preparation — and coach your team for the interviews. We regularly work inside clients' existing platforms. Tool plus judgment beats either alone.
