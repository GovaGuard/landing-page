// COMPARISON

HIPAA vs HITRUST

HIPAA HITRUST
What it is US federal law — Privacy, Security, and Breach Notification Rules Certifiable framework built on HIPAA, NIST, and ISO controls
Who demands it The law itself, plus every BAA counterparty Hospital systems and payer procurement
Output No certificate exists — compliance posture plus documented risk analysis HITRUST certification report, in e1/i1/r2 tiers
Effort Security Rule risk analysis and safeguards Substantial — evidence depth comparable to SOC 2 Type II and beyond
Renewal Continuous obligation 1–2 year certification cycles

There is no official "HIPAA certified" — anyone selling you one is selling paper. HITRUST exists precisely because buyers wanted a certifiable proxy for HIPAA compliance that an assessor has actually validated.

// LEVERAGE

Building on ISO 27001 and C5

If you already run an ISMS, most of the Security Rule is covered: access control, audit controls, integrity, and transmission security map cleanly onto an existing ISO 27001 or BSI C5 control set.

The deltas are US-specific — the formal HIPAA risk analysis the Security Rule mandates, BAA chain management across customers and subcontractors, breach notification clocks (60 days), minimum-necessary policies, and PHI-specific data flow documentation. We build exactly that delta on top of what you have, instead of standing up a second compliance program.

// EXECUTION

The engagement path

  1. HIPAA risk analysis

    The Security Rule's mandatory foundation — and the first thing OCR asks for after an incident. Scoped to your real PHI flows, not a generic asset inventory: where PHI enters, where it rests, who touches it, and which threats actually apply.

  2. Safeguards & policies

    Administrative, physical, and technical safeguards mapped onto your existing control set rather than written from scratch. Includes BAA templates and management of the subcontractor chain — every downstream processor of PHI needs its own agreement.

  3. Breach readiness

    Notification runbooks built around the 60-day federal clock, plus the state-law overlays that often run shorter. Who decides, who notifies, what evidence you preserve — rehearsed before you need it.

  4. HITRUST track

    Where procurement demands it: tier selection (e1, i1, or r2) against your actual deal requirements, readiness work against the chosen control set, and support through the validated assessment with an authorized external assessor.

// FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Is there an official HIPAA certification?

No. HHS certifies nothing and endorses no third-party seal. You demonstrate compliance through a documented risk analysis and implemented safeguards — that is what regulators and BAA counterparties examine. HITRUST is the certifiable proxy buyers accept when they want an assessor-validated report instead of your word.

Do we need HIPAA if we never see patient data?

If you touch PHI on behalf of a covered entity or another business associate — even encrypted, even transient — you are a business associate and HIPAA applies. Truly PHI-free architectures do exist, but that status needs to be proven with data flow analysis, not assumed because nobody intended to store patient data.

HITRUST e1, i1 or r2 — which one?

e1 covers 43 controls and works as an entry signal for smaller deals. i1 covers roughly 180 controls on an annual cycle and satisfies most mid-market procurement. r2 is tailored to your risk profile and is the heavyweight hospitals ask for. Start where your largest deal requires — the tiers build on each other, so nothing is wasted.

How does our GDPR work help?

Governance, risk discipline, and breach-response muscle transfer well. But HIPAA's BAA chain, the minimum-necessary standard, and US breach notification clocks are additional machinery with no GDPR equivalent. Plan a delta project on top of your existing program, not a relabel of it.
