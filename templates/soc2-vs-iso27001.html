SOC 2 vs ISO 27001: which one do you need?

The honest answer depends on who is asking you for proof — your buyers, not your preferences. If your pipeline is American, their vendor-risk teams will name SOC 2. If it is European, procurement will name ISO 27001. We run both programs for clients, so we have no horse in this race: the analysis below is what we tell companies before any engagement is on the table.

Reviewed by Nils Carstensen & Manuel Eckarth · September 2026

// VERDICT

The short answer

01

US enterprise buyers

SOC 2 Type II is what their vendor-risk teams expect and are staffed to review. Sending an ISO certificate instead usually triggers a longer questionnaire, not a shorter one. If your revenue is American, this is your answer.

  • Standard ask in US vendor risk reviews
  • Type II carries the weight; Type I rarely suffices
  • Report shared under NDA with each buyer
02

EU/DACH procurement & regulators

ISO 27001 is the recognized reference in European procurement and the baseline regulators point to. It also feeds directly into NIS2, DORA, and BSI C5 work — one ISMS carries all of them.

  • Named in EU tenders and framework contracts
  • Foundation for NIS2 / DORA compliance
  • Public certificate, no NDA required
03

Selling on both sides

One control set, both outputs. Build the ISMS once, attest twice: the same access, change, incident, and vendor controls satisfy the ISO auditor and the CPA firm. The mistake is treating them as two separate programs.

  • Single control set, single evidence base
  • Certificate for Europe, report for the US
  • Second attestation is incremental, not additive

// OVERLAP

When you need both

The overlap is the point. Access control, change management, incident response, vendor management — the controls map nearly one-to-one between the SOC 2 Security criteria and ISO 27001 Annex A.

The wrong move is running two parallel programs with two evidence sets, two audit calendars, and two versions of every policy; that is how compliance cost doubles for no additional security. The right move is one ISMS with one control set that produces a certificate for European buyers and a report for American ones.

This is how our ISO 27001 practice and our SOC 2 readiness service are deliberately built: same control library, same evidence repository, two attestations reading from it. The auditors differ; your controls should not.

// DECISION GUIDE

Decision guide

  1. US-only pipeline

    SOC 2 Type II, straight readiness path. Skip ISO 27001 until a European deal actually requires it — a certificate nobody asked for is pure carrying cost.

  2. EU-only pipeline

    ISO 27001. It clears procurement gates across the EU and it also carries NIS2/DORA weight, so the same investment covers your regulatory exposure, not just your sales cycle.

  3. Both markets

    ISO 27001 first as the system of record — the ISMS gives you the governance skeleton — then SOC 2 as an incremental attestation on the same controls. The reverse order works but usually costs more rework.

  4. German public sector or healthcare cloud

    Neither alone suffices. These buyers require BSI C5, typically on top of an ISO 27001 foundation. See our C5 practice (German) for that path.

// FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Is SOC 2 or ISO 27001 cheaper?

For a first program the total effort is comparable: readiness work dominates, and it is largely the same work. The cost profiles differ afterwards — SOC 2 recurs annually with a full re-attestation, while ISO 27001 runs on a three-year certification cycle with lighter annual surveillance audits. Over three years the costs converge. The genuinely expensive mistake is doing them as separate programs with duplicated controls and evidence.

Does SOC 2 replace ISO 27001 in Europe?

Rarely. European procurement teams and regulators reference ISO 27001 by name in tenders, contracts, and regulatory guidance. A SOC 2 report helps in a security review and may buy goodwill, but it usually does not clear the formal requirement — the checkbox says ISO 27001, and a restricted-distribution CPA report is not a certificate.

Can we convert one into the other?

There is no formal conversion — different auditors, different outputs, different rules. But a well-built control set means the second attestation reuses most of your evidence: on the SOC 2 Security criteria the overlap with ISO 27001 Annex A is typically 60–80%. In practice the second program is a delta exercise, not a restart.
