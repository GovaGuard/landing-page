The CRA CE-marking checklist: 15 months to December 2027

On 11 December 2027, the Cyber Resilience Act applies in full: no product with digital elements enters the EU market without a CE mark backed by the essential cybersecurity requirements. That is 15 months from now. Two deadlines have already passed — the notified-body framework applies since 11 June 2026, and the ENISA reporting obligations are live since 11 September 2026. This page is the checklist, quarter by quarter.

CE marking under the CRA is the manufacturer's declaration that a product with digital elements meets the essential cybersecurity requirements of Regulation (EU) 2024/2847 — Annex I — proven by a conformity assessment and a technical documentation file. Most products self-assess; Important Class I/II products face stricter routes, and Critical products require EU certification.

By Nils Carstensen & Manuel Eckarth · Last reviewed: September 17, 2026

// CHECKLIST

Eight items between you and the CE mark

The order matters. Classification decides your conformity route, the route decides whether you need a notified body, and notified-body capacity decides how much calendar you actually have. Work the list top-down; the quarters assume you start now.

  1. Product inventory & classification — Q4 2026

    List every product with digital elements you place on the EU market, then classify each: default, Important Class I or II, or Critical. Everything downstream — route, cost, timeline — hangs on this call. Document the reasoning per product.

  2. Conformity route decision — Q4 2026

    Default-class products self-assess. Important Class I/II products face stricter routes that can require a notified body; Critical products need EU certification. Decide the route per product now, because the routes have very different lead times.

  3. ENISA reporting runbook — already overdue

    Since 11 September 2026 you must send an early warning to ENISA within 24 hours of becoming aware of an actively exploited vulnerability, and a full notification within 72 hours — including for products already on the market. If this runbook does not exist yet, it goes first.

  4. Notified-body booking where needed — Q1 2027

    The framework applies since 11 June 2026 and assessor capacity is finite. If any product needs third-party assessment, book the slot before you finish the gap work — a perfect technical file with no assessor available does not ship in December 2027.

  5. Annex I Part I gap assessment — Q1 2027

    Map each product against the essential requirements: no known exploitable vulnerabilities at release, secure-by-default configuration, minimized attack surface. The output is a prioritized engineering backlog per product — sized early enough to fit into 2027 release trains.

  6. Vulnerability-handling process — Q1–Q2 2027

    Annex I Part II: an SBOM for every product, a published coordinated vulnerability disclosure policy, and a patch pipeline that holds for the whole support period. Wire SBOM generation into the build — we cover how in SBOM generation in CI.

  7. Support-period policy — Q2 2027

    Commit, per product, to a support period with security updates — at least five years where users can reasonably expect it. State it publicly, budget the maintenance, and make end-of-support dates part of the product record.

  8. Technical documentation file — Q2–Q3 2027

    The cybersecurity risk assessment and the technical file that back the CE mark: design, vulnerability handling, test evidence, the EU declaration of conformity. Draft it as the gap work closes, not after — leaving Q4 2027 for assessment and affixing the mark.

// ALREADY BINDING

The September 2026 trap

Most roadmaps were built around December 2027. The reporting duty arrived fifteen months earlier: since 11 September 2026, an actively exploited vulnerability in your product triggers a 24-hour early warning and a 72-hour notification to ENISA — legacy products on the market included. This is not a future checklist item; it is a live legal obligation.

The trap is capability, not paperwork. If you cannot detect that a vulnerability in your product is being exploited, you cannot report it in 24 hours — the clock starts when you become aware, but awareness itself takes monitoring, intake channels, and triage that most product teams have never built. Test the runbook against a realistic scenario before a real one tests it for you.

// FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Which CRA obligations already apply today?

Two. Since 11 June 2026 the notified-body framework applies, so conformity assessors can be designated and booked. Since 11 September 2026 the reporting obligations are live: actively exploited vulnerabilities and severe incidents go to ENISA — early warning within 24 hours, full notification within 72 hours — including for products already on the market.

Does every product need a notified body?

No. Default-class products — the majority — self-assess conformity. Important products in Class I and II face stricter routes that can involve a notified body, and Critical products require EU certification. That is why classification is step one: it decides whether you need external assessment capacity at all.

What happens if my product has no CE mark on 11 December 2027?

It can no longer be placed on the EU market. Market-surveillance authorities can order withdrawal of non-conforming products, and fines reach €15 million or 2.5% of global annual turnover, whichever is higher. Existing units already sold are not recalled by the deadline itself, but new placements stop.

Is 15 months enough time?

For most default-class products, yes — if classification and the Annex I gap assessment happen in the next two quarters. For products needing a notified body, the constraint is assessor capacity, not your own backlog: book early. What no longer fits in the timeline is starting the reporting capability late — that obligation is already binding.
