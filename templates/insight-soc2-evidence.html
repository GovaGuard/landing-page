The SOC 2 evidence list: what auditors actually request

A typical first-year SOC 2 Type II involves evidence requests spanning the five Trust Services Criteria categories defined by the AICPA: Security — mandatory, with common criteria CC1–CC9 — plus Availability, Processing Integrity, Confidentiality, and Privacy where in scope. The requests follow predictable patterns, which is exactly what our SOC 2 readiness service prepares clients for: the table below maps what auditors ask, what artifacts satisfy them, and what you should automate.

SOC 2 evidence is the set of documents, system exports, screenshots, tickets, and logs a CPA firm collects to verify that your controls existed (Type I) or operated effectively across a 3–12 month observation window (Type II). Auditors sample it against the Trust Services Criteria; gaps become exceptions in the final report.

// EVIDENCE MAP

Seven evidence categories, one request list

Vulnerability & endpoint management Proof of scanning, patching within defined SLAs, and managed, encrypted endpoints Scanner reports, remediation tickets, MDM compliance exports, penetration test report Automate scans and MDM; pen test stays a manual annual artifact
Monitoring & incident response Evidence that alerts fire, incidents are triaged, and postmortems happen Alerting configuration, on-call schedules, incident tickets with timelines, IR plan and test Mixed — alert exports automate; postmortems and IR tests are manual
Vendor management Proof that critical vendors are inventoried, risk-assessed, and reviewed Vendor register, subservice organization SOC reports, DPAs, annual review records Manual — tooling tracks it, but assessments need human judgment
HR & policies Evidence of background checks, policy acknowledgments, and security training Signed policies, acknowledgment logs, training completion exports, screening confirmations Automate acknowledgments and training via HRIS or GRC tooling
Backup & availability (if in scope) Proof that backups run, restores are tested, and capacity and failover are managed Backup job logs, restore test records, DR test report, uptime and capacity dashboards Automate job logs; restore and DR tests remain scheduled manual exercises

The first four categories map primarily to the Security common criteria (CC6–CC8 for access, change, and operations) and apply to every report. The backup and availability row only becomes an audit topic when Availability is added as a criterion — Security is the sole mandatory category; the other four are optional additions.

// HOW IT IS TESTED

Populations, samples, and the observation window

For a Type II report, auditors do not read everything. They request a population — every production change, every new hire, every incident in the 3–12 month observation window — then select a sample and ask for the full evidence trail on each item. An incomplete population is worse than a failed sample: it undermines trust in everything else you submit.

Three habits keep the window clean. First, generate evidence where work happens — approvals in the pull request, offboarding in the ticket system — instead of reconstructing it later. Second, prefer system-generated exports with visible timestamps over screenshots. Third, run an internal evidence dry run mid-window, so gaps surface while you can still fix the process rather than explain the exception.

Who asks for all this matters too: request volume and sampling style vary noticeably between audit firms. If you are a European company buying a US-market report, our guide to choosing a CPA firm from Europe covers how to compare them before you sign.

// FAQ

Frequently asked questions

How many evidence requests should we expect?

In GovaGuard practice experience, a first-year Security-only Type II commonly lands between roughly 100 and 250 individual requests, depending on the CPA firm and system complexity. Each additional Trust Services category adds its own criteria and therefore its own requests — one reason to scope extra categories deliberately rather than by default.

Do we need evidence for all five Trust Services Criteria?

No. Only Security — the common criteria CC1–CC9 — is mandatory in every SOC 2 report. Availability, Processing Integrity, Confidentiality, and Privacy are optional and belong in scope only when customer commitments demand them. Evidence follows scope: the backup and availability category in our table matters only if Availability is included.

Are screenshots acceptable evidence?

Usually yes, but they are the weakest form. Auditors prefer system-generated exports with timestamps, record counts, and the query or filter visible, because screenshots are easy to stage and hard to tie to a complete population. Use screenshots for configuration states that cannot be exported; use exports for anything list-shaped.

Does GRC automation tooling replace evidence work?

It removes the collection grind for API-connected systems — access lists, MDM status, training completion — but not the controls themselves. Vendor assessments, incident postmortems, restore tests, and access review sign-offs still require humans to do the work the evidence documents. Tooling shortens the audit; it does not pass it for you.
