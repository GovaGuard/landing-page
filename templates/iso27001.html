// EXECUTION

The engagement path

  1. Gap analysis

    Two to three weeks. We define the certification scope — the single decision that most determines cost — map your current state against the Annex A controls of ISO/IEC 27001, and draft your Statement of Applicability. You leave with a prioritized backlog and a realistic timeline, not a red-amber-green slide.

  2. ISMS build

    Risk assessment and treatment plan first; policies second. We write policies that describe how your organization actually works, then close the control gaps: access management, supplier reviews, logging, incident response, business continuity. Generic templates fail audits and get ignored by engineers — we don't use them.

  3. Internal audit & management review

    Both are mandatory before certification. We run the internal audit with auditor independence, log the nonconformities, drive corrective actions, and prepare leadership for a management review that satisfies Clause 9 — because the certification auditor will read those minutes.

  4. Certification audit support

    We help you select an accredited certification body, then support Stage 1 (documentation review) and Stage 2 (implementation audit) — preparing interviewees, staging evidence, and handling findings until the certificate is issued.

Timeline & cost expectations

For a company of 20–200 people with a sane scope, expect four to eight months from kickoff to certificate. The variance comes from scope breadth, how much of your infrastructure is already documented, and how quickly your team closes control gaps.

// FAQ

Frequently asked questions

How long does ISO 27001 certification take?

Four to eight months for most startups and scale-ups: two to three weeks of gap analysis, two to four months of ISMS build, then internal audit, management review, and the two-stage certification audit. Larger scopes or heavy legacy infrastructure push toward the upper end. Claims of significantly less usually mean the ISMS exists only on paper.

What does ISO 27001 cost for a startup?

Three components: consulting (fixed-scope, quoted after the gap analysis), certification body fees of roughly €5,000–€15,000 for SMEs including surveillance audits, and internal effort of roughly 0.2–0.5 FTE during the build. GRC platforms add €10k–€40k per year and are optional — useful for evidence automation, not a substitute for the work.

Do we need a full-time CISO for ISO 27001?

No. The standard requires that information security responsibilities are assigned and that top management demonstrably leads the ISMS — it does not require a full-time CISO title. A named internal owner backed by external security expertise satisfies auditors and is how most certified companies under 200 people actually operate. We support exactly this setup throughout the engagement.

Is ISO 27001 enough for US customers?

Often US enterprise buyers ask for a SOC 2 Type II report instead of, or in addition to, ISO 27001. The control overlap is substantial — access control, change management, incident response, vendor management largely map one to one. We design the ISMS so the same controls and evidence serve both, which makes a later SOC 2 an incremental effort rather than a second program. See SOC 2 vs ISO 27001 for the full comparison.
